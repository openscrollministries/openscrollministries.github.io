Second Sunday of Advent

Sunday, December 6, 2026. Comfort that tells the truth, and a voice crying in the wilderness.

The readings

First reading
Isaiah 40:1–11
Psalm
Psalm 85:1–2, 8–13
Second reading
2 Peter 3:8–15a
Gospel
Mark 1:1–8

Psalm 85 is read in two stretches, verses 1–2 and verses 8–13. The second reading ends in the middle of 2 Peter 3:15, at the words about the Lord’s patience as salvation.

Source: Advent, Year B, Vanderbilt Divinity Library.

Gospel: Mark 1:1–8

World English Bible, public domain.

1 The beginning of the Good News of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.

2 As it is written in the prophets,
“Behold, I send my messenger before your face,
who will prepare your way before you:

3 the voice of one crying in the wilderness,
‘Make ready the way of the Lord!
Make his paths straight!’”

4 John came baptizing in the wilderness and preaching the baptism of repentance for forgiveness of sins.

5 All the country of Judea and all those of Jerusalem went out to him. They were baptized by him in the Jordan river, confessing their sins.

6 John was clothed with camel’s hair and a leather belt around his waist. He ate locusts and wild honey.

7 He preached, saying, “After me comes he who is mightier than I, the thong of whose sandals I am not worthy to stoop down and loosen.

8 I baptized you in water, but he will baptize you in the Holy Spirit.”

A note for study

The second Sunday of Advent offers a comfort that tells the truth.

Isaiah 40 opens with a doubled word. Comfort, comfort my people. Speak comfortably to Jerusalem: her warfare is accomplished, her iniquity pardoned. Then a voice calls, and the comfort turns into roadwork. Prepare the way of the Lord in the wilderness. Make a level highway in the desert. Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain made low, until his glory is revealed and all flesh sees it. The prophet is not told to pretend the ground is already level. All flesh is like grass. The grass withers, the flower fades, and the word of our God stands forever. The reading ends with strength and tenderness together. The Lord will come as a mighty one, and he will feed his flock like a shepherd, gathering the lambs in his arm. Comfort, here, is the strong kindness of a God who comes.

Psalm 85 remembers that the Lord has forgiven the iniquity of his people, then moves to what his coming looks like. Mercy and truth meet together. Righteousness and peace have kissed each other. The psalm will not hand us a peace that leaves wrongs where they are. The peace of Advent arrives when God sets things right.

Second Peter 3 is for the year when the promise feels late. The Lord is not slow concerning his promise, as some count slowness. He is patient, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance. One day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day. Believers look for new heavens and a new earth, in which righteousness dwells, and they are told to be diligent to be found in peace. The reading stops halfway through verse 15: regard the patience of our Lord as salvation. The time we call delay is time someone else is being given.

Mark opens his Gospel by stepping onto Isaiah’s road. This is the beginning of the good news of Jesus Christ, the Son of God. A messenger is sent ahead. A voice cries in the wilderness: make ready the way of the Lord, make his paths straight. John comes, baptizing in the wilderness and preaching a baptism of repentance for forgiveness of sins, and Judea goes out to him, confessing. He is clothed with camel’s hair, and his sermon is about someone else. After him comes one who is mightier, the thong of whose sandals John is not worthy to loosen. John baptizes in water. The Coming One will baptize in the Holy Spirit.

Isaiah’s highway and Mark’s opening quotation are the same road. Comfort does not skip repentance. The comfortable word to Jerusalem and the baptism in the Jordan belong to one announcement: God is near, so make his paths straight. The second candle is often named Peace. These readings say that peace is on the way, and that the way itself still needs preparing.

For a small group

  1. Isaiah pairs a word of comfort with a command to level a highway. Where do we want the comfort without the roadwork?
  2. Psalm 85 says mercy and truth meet, and righteousness and peace kiss. What kind of peace is that, and what kind of peace does it rule out?
  3. Peter calls the Lord’s patience salvation. Whose repentance might still be the reason we are waiting?
  4. John spends his sermon on someone else. What would it look like for this group to prepare a way rather than draw a crowd?

A closing prayer

God of all comfort, speak your word to us until it stands when everything else fades. Level in us what is proud, lift what has fallen, and make us ready for your Son. Give us peace that belongs with righteousness, through Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one God, now and forever. Amen.
