Fourth Sunday of Advent

Sunday, December 20, 2026. God will make David a house. Mary says yes.

The readings

First reading
2 Samuel 7:1–11, 16
Psalm
Luke 1:46b–55, or Psalm 89:1–4, 19–26
Second reading
Romans 16:25–27
Gospel
Luke 1:26–38

The Old Testament reading moves from 2 Samuel 7:11 to verse 16, from God’s answer to David’s building plan to the promise that David’s house will be made sure forever. In the psalm column, Mary’s song is listed first, and Psalm 89:1–4, 19–26 may be used instead. A church that sang the Magnificat on the third Sunday can sing Psalm 89 today.

Source: Advent, Year B, Vanderbilt Divinity Library.

Gospel: Luke 1:26–38

World English Bible, public domain.

26 Now in the sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent from God to a city of Galilee, named Nazareth,

27 to a virgin pledged to be married to a man whose name was Joseph, of David’s house. The virgin’s name was Mary.

28 Having come in, the angel said to her, “Rejoice, you highly favored one! The Lord is with you. Blessed are you among women!”

29 But when she saw him, she was greatly troubled at the saying, and considered what kind of salutation this might be.

30 The angel said to her, “Don’t be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God.

31 Behold, you will conceive in your womb, and give birth to a son, and will call his name ‘Jesus.’

32 He will be great, and will be called the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give him the throne of his father, David,

33 and he will reign over the house of Jacob forever. There will be no end to his Kingdom.”

34 Mary said to the angel, “How can this be, seeing I am a virgin?”

35 The angel answered her, “The Holy Spirit will come on you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore also the holy one who is born from you will be called the Son of God.

36 Behold, Elizabeth, your relative, also has conceived a son in her old age; and this is the sixth month with her who was called barren.

37 For nothing spoken by God is impossible.”

38 Mary said, “Behold, the servant of the Lord; let it be done to me according to your word.”
The angel departed from her.

A note for study

On the fourth Sunday, the promise gets an address.

Second Samuel 7 carries the rest of the story. David sits in a house of cedar while the ark of God is still in a tent, and he means to build something better. Nathan agrees. In the night, God corrects them both. God never asked for cedar. He took David from the pasture and will plant his people so they are disturbed no more. The reading then steps from verse 11 to verse 16: God will make David a house, and his kingdom will be made sure forever. What this Sunday holds up is the promise that does not end.

That is why the psalm column offers Mary’s song or Psalm 89. Psalm 89 sings the covenant with David: mercy that lasts, a throne for every generation, a servant raised up and anointed. Mary’s song sings the same covenant from a village. The mighty one has done great things. He has helped Israel his servant, remembering mercy, as he spoke to Abraham and his offspring forever. A church that sang the Magnificat last week can sing Psalm 89 today. A church that did not can sing Mary’s song beside the story of how she received it.

Romans 16:25–27 is easy to treat as a closing formality. Paul will not allow it. The gospel was a mystery kept secret for long ages and now disclosed through the prophetic writings, so that all the nations might come to the obedience of faith. The promise to David was never a private family matter. It was always aimed outward. Glory to the only wise God, through Jesus Christ, forever.

Then Gabriel is sent to Nazareth, to a virgin pledged to Joseph, of David’s house. Her name is Mary. She is troubled, and she asks how this can be. The angel names the child more than he explains the means. She will bear a son and call him Jesus. He will be great, the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give him the throne of his father David, and of his kingdom there will be no end. The house David was not permitted to build stands in front of her, still only a word. The Holy Spirit will come on her, and the holy one born of her will be called the Son of God. Nothing spoken by God is impossible. Elizabeth, old and once called barren, is already six months along, a sign close to home that God has not stopped keeping promises.

Mary’s answer is what the church has been trying to learn for four weeks. Behold, the servant of the Lord. Let it be done to me according to your word. The fourth candle is often named Love. In this story love is not a feeling she works up. It is her yes to a promise older than she is, kept at last in a child she cannot yet hold.

For a small group

  1. David wants to build a house for God, and God says he will build a house for David. Where do we try to do for God what God means to do for us?
  2. The lectionary jumps from verse 11 to verse 16 and lands on a kingdom made sure forever. What would it mean to trust a promise we cannot yet see built?
  3. Mary asks how this can be, and then she consents. What is the difference between her question and a refusal?
  4. Romans says the old mystery is now told so that the nations might obey in faith. Who, beyond our own household, is this promise for?

A closing prayer

Lord God, you took David from the pasture and promised him a house that would stand forever. Give us Mary’s courage to ask how, and Mary’s freedom to say yes. Let your word be done among us, through Jesus Christ, Son of David and Son of God, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one God, now and forever. Amen.
