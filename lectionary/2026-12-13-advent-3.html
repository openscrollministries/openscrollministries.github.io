Third Sunday of Advent

Sunday, December 13, 2026. Joy, because the Light already has a witness.

The readings

First reading
Isaiah 61:1–4, 8–11
Psalm
Psalm 126, or Luke 1:46b–55
Second reading
1 Thessalonians 5:16–24
Gospel
John 1:6–8, 19–28

The psalm column offers a choice. A church may sing Psalm 126, or the Song of Mary in Luke 1:46b–55. The Gospel skips from John 1:8 to John 1:19.

Gospel: John 1:6–8, 19–28

A note for study

This is the Sunday of joy, and the texts are careful about what the joy is for.

Isaiah 61 speaks in the voice of someone the Spirit of the Lord is upon. He is anointed to preach good news to the humble, to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and to comfort all who mourn. Mourners receive a garland for ashes and the oil of joy for mourning. They are called trees of righteousness, the Lord’s own planting. Ruined cities find repairers. God loves justice, hates robbery, and promises an everlasting covenant, until joy springs up like a garden. This joy is not a decoration on an unchanged world. It is the sound a restored people make.

The psalm column offers two songs. Psalm 126 remembers a restoration that felt like a dream, then tells the truth about now: those who sow in tears reap in joy. Mary’s song, the other choice, magnifies the Lord because he has looked at the humble state of his servant. He scatters the proud, puts down princes from their thrones, exalts the lowly, fills the hungry, and sends the rich away empty. He helps Israel his servant, remembering mercy, as he spoke to Abraham and his offspring forever. Both songs refuse a thin joy. Tears are still in the furrow. Joy is what God does for people who cannot arrange it.

First Thessalonians 5:16–24 is where the church learned to say “rejoice always” without pretending the week was easy. Rejoice always. Pray without ceasing. In everything give thanks. Do not quench the Spirit. Test all things, and hold firmly what is good. Then Paul prays what no class can do for itself. May the God of peace sanctify you completely, and keep spirit, soul, and body blameless at the coming of the Lord Jesus. He who calls you is faithful, who will also do it. Joy is commanded, and joy is promised, because the God who calls is the God who finishes.

John 1 gives that joy a witness who will not take the title. John is sent from God to testify about the light, so that all might believe through him. He is not the light. Asked who he is, he is not the Christ, not Elijah, and not the prophet. He is a voice crying in the wilderness, “Make straight the way of the Lord.” Asked why he baptizes, he answers with the sentence for this Sunday. Among you stands one whom you do not know.

The lectionary leaves out verses 9 through 18, the great lines about the true light. The gap fits this Sunday. We are still in the wilderness, still being told to make a straight way, and told to rejoice, because the one we do not yet see clearly is already standing among us. The God who called us will do what he said.

For a small group

  1. Isaiah’s joy comes with good news for the humble and liberty for captives. What would false joy sound like in our congregation, and what would this joy sound like?
  2. Psalm 126 and Mary’s song both tell of a reversal. Which line is hardest to believe in our own town, and why?
  3. Paul says to rejoice always and also that the God who calls us is faithful and will do it. Which of those two do we need to hear first this week?
  4. John refuses every title and points to someone already standing among the people, unrecognized. Where might we be missing Christ because we are busy deciding who he is not?

A closing prayer

God of peace, you anoint your servants with good news for the humble and comfort for those who mourn. Teach us to rejoice without lying about the week, and keep spirit, soul, and body blameless at the coming of your Son. You are faithful, and you will do it, through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.
