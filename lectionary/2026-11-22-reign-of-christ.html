Christ the King

Sunday, November 22, 2026. Reign of Christ, the last Sunday of Year A.

The readings

First reading
Ezekiel 34:11–16, 20–24
Psalm
Psalm 100, or Psalm 95:1–7a
Second reading
Ephesians 1:15–23
Gospel
Matthew 25:31–46

Vanderbilt lists Psalm 100 with the first reading and Psalm 95:1–7a as the alternate psalm. The Old Testament lesson is the same in both columns: Ezekiel 34:11–16, 20–24.

Source: Year A, after Pentecost, Vanderbilt Divinity Library.

Gospel: Matthew 25:31–46

World English Bible, public domain.

31 “But when the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then he will sit on the throne of his glory.

32 Before him all the nations will be gathered, and he will separate them one from another, as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats.

33 He will set the sheep on his right hand, but the goats on the left.

34 Then the King will tell those on his right hand, ‘Come, blessed of my Father, inherit the Kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world;

35 for I was hungry, and you gave me food to eat. I was thirsty, and you gave me drink. I was a stranger, and you took me in.

36 I was naked, and you clothed me. I was sick, and you visited me. I was in prison, and you came to me.’

37 “Then the righteous will answer him, saying, ‘Lord, when did we see you hungry, and feed you; or thirsty, and give you a drink?

38 When did we see you as a stranger, and take you in; or naked, and clothe you?

39 When did we see you sick, or in prison, and come to you?’

40 “The King will answer them, ‘Most certainly I tell you, because you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me.’

41 Then he will say also to those on the left hand, ‘Depart from me, you cursed, into the eternal fire which is prepared for the devil and his angels;

42 for I was hungry, and you didn’t give me food to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me no drink;

43 I was a stranger, and you didn’t take me in; naked, and you didn’t clothe me; sick, and in prison, and you didn’t visit me.’

44 “Then they will also answer, saying, ‘Lord, when did we see you hungry, or thirsty, or a stranger, or naked, or sick, or in prison, and didn’t help you?’

45 “Then he will answer them, saying, ‘Most certainly I tell you, because you didn’t do it to one of the least of these, you didn’t do it to me.’

46 These will go away into eternal punishment, but the righteous into eternal life.”

A note for study

The church year ends with a throne, and with a shepherd.

Ezekiel 34 has already told the truth about shepherds who fed themselves and let the flock scatter. God takes the work back. He will seek the lost, bring back the strayed, bind up the injured, and strengthen the weak, and judge between the sheep who pushed and the sheep who were pushed aside. Then he promises one shepherd, David his servant, and says that he himself will be their God. The King these readings confess is not a late idea. He is the shepherd God promised when the shepherds had failed.

Psalm 100 answers from inside the flock. The people come into his gates with thanksgiving, the sheep of his pasture. Psalm 95, the alternate, makes the same claim and adds a warning: hear his voice. Either way, the God who reigns is the God who keeps a people.

Ephesians 1 locates him now. Paul thanks God for a church’s faith and love, then prays that they will know the hope of their calling. The power he points to raised Christ from the dead and seated him above every rule and authority, in this age and the age to come. All things are under his feet, and he is head over all things for the church, his body. Other thrones are real. None of them outranks him, and the church is joined to his reign rather than watching it from a distance.

Matthew 25 shows that King doing a shepherd’s work at the end. The nations gather. Sheep go to the right and goats to the left, and both groups are surprised. The righteous cannot remember serving him. The others cannot remember refusing him. Hunger, thirst, estrangement, nakedness, sickness, and prison were the meeting place. Jesus calls these “the least of these my brothers.” Some readers hear the disciples and the suffering church. Others hear every person in need. The disagreement should be named honestly. Then tell the truth either reading requires: the King is present where people are easy to overlook, and mercy to them is mercy to him.

This is not a way to earn the kingdom. The sheep inherit what was prepared from the foundation of the world. They are blessed before they are praised for a visit to a prison. Mercy is fruit, and fruit still matters. A church can say that Christ reigns and still walk past the people in whom he says he may be met. The last Sunday of Year A is a day to ask for grace to live as this shepherd’s flock.

For a small group

  1. Ezekiel says God will both seek the lost and judge between sheep. Where do you need seeking right now, and where do you need that kind of truth?
  2. In Matthew 25 the people who showed mercy are surprised. What does their surprise suggest about how mercy actually grows in a life?
  3. Ephesians says Christ is head over all things for the church. How should that change the way a congregation looks at someone who is hungry, sick, or in prison?
  4. As this church year ends, who have we walked past while we were busy calling Jesus Lord?

A closing prayer

Shepherd of your people, you promised to seek the lost and to judge the flock with equity. As this year ends, give us eyes for the people we overlook, and make our mercy a true sign that we belong to your Son, Jesus Christ, who reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one God, now and forever. Amen.
