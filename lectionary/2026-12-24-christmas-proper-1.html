Weekly Lectionary · Year B · Nativity of the Lord, Proper I

Christmas Eve and Christmas Day

Thursday, December 24, and Friday, December 25, 2026. Proper I, the readings of the Nativity. A child is born, and the child is King.

The readings

First reading
Isaiah 9:2–7
Psalm
Psalm 96
Second reading
Titus 2:11–14
Gospel
Luke 2:1–14, with verses 15–20 optional

Vanderbilt titles this set “Nativity of the Lord, Proper I,” and notes that it may be read on Christmas Eve, in the morning, or at midday. Proper II and Proper III are different sets, for the same feast. This page stays with Proper I.

Source: Christmas, Year B, Vanderbilt Divinity Library.

Gospel: Luke 2:1–14

World English Bible, public domain.

1 Now in those days, a decree went out from Caesar Augustus that all the world should be enrolled.

2 This was the first enrollment made when Quirinius was governor of Syria.

3 All went to enroll themselves, everyone to his own city.

4 Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judea, to David’s city, which is called Bethlehem, because he was of the house and family of David;

5 to enroll himself with Mary, who was pledged to be married to him as wife, being pregnant.

6 While they were there, the day had come for her to give birth.

7 She gave birth to her firstborn son. She wrapped him in bands of cloth, and laid him in a feeding trough, because there was no room for them in the inn.

8 There were shepherds in the same country staying in the field, and keeping watch by night over their flock.

9 Behold, an angel of the Lord stood by them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were terrified.

10 The angel said to them, “Don’t be afraid, for behold, I bring you good news of great joy which will be to all the people.

11 For there is born to you today, in David’s city, a Savior, who is Christ the Lord.

12 This is the sign to you: you will find a baby wrapped in strips of cloth, lying in a feeding trough.”

13 Suddenly, there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly army praising God, and saying,

14 “Glory to God in the highest,
on earth peace, good will toward men.”

Verses 15–20 are optional in the lectionary.

15 When the angels went away from them into the sky, the shepherds said to one another, “Let’s go to Bethlehem, now, and see this thing that has happened, which the Lord has made known to us.”

16 They came with haste, and found both Mary and Joseph, and the baby was lying in the feeding trough.

17 When they saw it, they publicized widely the saying which was spoken to them about this child.

18 All who heard it wondered at the things which were spoken to them by the shepherds.

19 But Mary kept all these sayings, pondering them in her heart.

20 The shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all the things that they had heard and seen, just as it was told them.

A note for study

Proper I is the church’s Christmas Eve reading, and it may be used in the morning or at midday as well. The texts say that a child has been born, and that the child is King.

Isaiah 9 looks at people who walked in darkness and have seen a great light. Before the child is named, the prophet says what his coming undoes: the burdened yoke and the oppressor’s rod are broken, and garments rolled in blood become fuel for a fire. Then the sentence the feast has never improved on, in the World English Bible: “For to us a child is born. To us a son is given; and the government will be on his shoulders. His name will be called Wonderful, Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.” Of the increase of his government and of peace there shall be no end, on David’s throne, upheld with justice and with righteousness forever. God’s own zeal will perform this. Isaiah does not hand the work to a better program.

Psalm 96 is larger than a lullaby. Sing a new song. Declare his glory among the nations. The gods of the peoples are idols, and the Lord made the heavens. The psalm ends where Christmas and Advent meet. The Lord reigns, and he is coming to judge the earth with righteousness and the peoples with his truth. The child and the judge are not two stories. The light Isaiah sees is the reign of a king whose justice is good news.

Titus 2:11–14 keeps the night from becoming only a memory. The grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all people, and that grace trains us. It teaches a people to renounce ungodliness and to live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present age, while they wait for the blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ. He gave himself to redeem a people of his own, zealous for good works. Grace has appeared. Glory will appear. Between the two, a people is being trained.

Luke 2 sets that promise inside Caesar’s enrollment of the world. Joseph and Mary go up to Bethlehem, and she lays her firstborn in a feeding trough because there is no room in the inn. The first to hear are shepherds keeping watch. The news is great joy for all the people: today, in David’s city, a Savior, who is Christ the Lord. The sign is a baby in a feeding trough, and then heaven’s army fills the sky with glory to God and peace on earth.

Verses 15 through 20 are optional, and worth keeping when the service stops at the song. The shepherds go and see. Mary ponders the sayings. They return glorifying God. Caesar can enroll the world. He cannot give it a Savior. Isaiah’s child, David’s heir, and Mary’s son are one announcement, and the throne promised in the dark is a feeding trough in Bethlehem.

For a small group

  1. Isaiah says the government will be on this child’s shoulders, and that peace will have no end. What kind of peace is he announcing, if garments rolled in blood are fuel for a fire?
  2. Psalm 96 ends with the Lord coming to judge the earth. How does that belong in a Christmas service, rather than only in Advent?
  3. Titus says grace has appeared, and that we are still waiting for glory to appear. What is grace training us to leave behind, and what is it training us to love?
  4. The sign given to the shepherds is a baby in a feeding trough. Why is smallness the sign, and what would we have preferred instead?

A closing prayer

Glory to you, God in the highest. You have shined on people who walked in darkness, and you have given us a child who is Savior, Christ the Lord. Train us by your grace while we wait for his glory, and let your peace rest on this world, through Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one God, now and forever. Amen.
