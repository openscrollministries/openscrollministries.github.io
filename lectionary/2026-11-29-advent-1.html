First Sunday of Advent

Sunday, November 29, 2026. The new year opens with a prayer that God will come, and a command to keep watch.

The readings

First reading
Isaiah 64:1–9
Psalm
Psalm 80:1–7, 17–19
Second reading
1 Corinthians 1:3–9
Gospel
Mark 13:24–37

In the Hebrew Bible, the lines English Bibles number as the start of Isaiah 64 still belong to the end of Isaiah 63. The Revised Common Lectionary follows the English numbering, Isaiah 64:1–9. Psalm 80 is read in two parts: the opening plea, and the closing plea in verses 17–19.

Source: Advent, Year B, Vanderbilt Divinity Library.

Gospel: Mark 13:24–37

World English Bible, public domain.

25 the stars will be falling from the sky, and the powers that are in the heavens will be shaken.

26 Then they will see the Son of Man coming in clouds with great power and glory.

A note for study

Advent does not open on a cradle. It opens on a prayer that God will come, and on a command to stay awake. With this Sunday, Year B begins.

Isaiah 64 is the prayer of people who know they cannot rescue themselves. They ask God to tear the heavens open and come down, the way he came down once before. They also refuse to flatter the congregation. Everyone has become unclean, and all their righteousness is like a polluted garment. Then the prophet puts them back in the oldest trust Israel has. The Lord is their Father. They are the clay, and he is the potter. We are all the work of his hand. Advent hope is not the claim that we are ready. It is the claim that God is still our Father when we are not.

Psalm 80 prays in the voice of a scattered people. Restore us. Let your face shine, that we may be saved. The church does not ask God to admire its wreaths. It asks him to turn his face toward us and bring us home.

First Corinthians 1:3–9 keeps that prayer from sinking into despair. Paul is writing to a church he will soon have to correct, and he begins with thanks. They are waiting for the revelation of the Lord Jesus Christ, who will confirm them until the end, blameless in his day. God is faithful. Through him they were called into the fellowship of his Son. The one we wait for is not a stranger, and his faithfulness, not our alertness, is what holds the church.

Mark 13 is why the Sunday still feels like a watch. After the oppression, the sun goes dark, the stars fall, and the Son of Man comes in clouds with great power and glory to gather his chosen from the four winds. Then he will not give a date. No one knows that day or hour, not the angels, nor the Son, only the Father. The fig tree says a season can be recognized. The household story says the hour cannot. A man goes away, leaves each servant a task, and tells the doorkeeper to keep watch. What Jesus tells them, he tells all: Watch.

The four readings are one posture. Isaiah asks God to come. The psalm asks God to restore. Paul says God will, because God is faithful. Jesus says the coming is certain and the hour is hidden, so the faithful work is not prediction. It is a household that stays awake, each person at the work they were given. The first Advent candle is often named Hope. In these texts hope is not a mood. It is clay in the potter’s hands, waiting for God, and refusing to sleep through the wait.

For a small group

  1. Isaiah asks God to come down, and then confesses that the people’s best deeds are like a polluted garment. How can both sentences be prayed in the same week?
  2. Paul says God is faithful and will confirm the church until the end. What changes in our watching if the watch depends on his faithfulness rather than on our nerve?
  3. Jesus says the season can be recognized and the hour cannot. Where are we tempted to demand a timetable instead of staying at our post?
  4. If “watch” means each person keeping the work they were given, what is the work in front of this group between now and Christmas?

A closing prayer

Father of your people, we are the clay and you are the potter. Tear open our slow hearts, restore us, and keep us awake for the coming of your Son. You are faithful, and we wait for him, Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, one God, now and forever. Amen.
