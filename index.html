Biblical archaeology briefing

What the field is finding

Last updated October 8, 2026. Summaries are written from published reports, with sources linked. Interpretations of finds are often debated, and the notes say so where they are.

September 2026Kiryat Gat, Israel · Roman period

A 25-ton Jewish ritual bath is moved intact

The Israel Antiquities Authority announced on September 17 that it had moved an intact mikvah found at Kiryat Gat to a conservation facility while construction and preservation work continue. Engineers reinforced the base with steel beams and treated the plaster first. The bath is about 13 feet (4 m) wide. It belonged to a Jewish village that was abandoned during the Bar Kokhba revolt.

Bible connection. Ritual immersion pools are common in Jewish settlements of the Second Temple and Roman periods, the world of the Gospels and Acts.

Source: Bible Archaeology Report, September roundup

September 2026Hattusa, Turkey · about 1269 BC

A piece of the Treaty of Kadesh turns up in the Hittite capital

Archaeologists excavating a building at Hattusa found a cuneiform tablet fragment that preserves part of the Treaty of Kadesh, according to Turkey's Ministry of Culture and Tourism. The treaty was made around 1269 BC between Ramesses II of Egypt and the Hittite king Hattusili III, and it is among the best-known peace agreements of the ancient world. The reported fragment includes provisions about people seeking refuge across borders.

Bible connection. Late Bronze Age treaties like this one are often compared with the structure of the covenants in Exodus and Deuteronomy. That comparison is a scholarly one, not something the report itself claims.

Source: Bible Archaeology Report, September roundup

August 2026Rome, Italy · about AD 39

Low water on the Tiber and a possible link to Sergius Paulus

A dry spell exposed the remains of an ancient bridge in the Tiber at Rome. It has long been called Nero's Bridge, though many scholars think Caligula began it around AD 39. An inscription found in 1887 names five commissioners of the Tiber, including Lucius Sergius Paullus. Bryan Windle of the Associates for Biblical Research argues that this man may be the proconsul Sergius Paulus whom Paul met on Cyprus. The identification is possible, not proven.

Bible connection. Acts 13:6–12 describes Paul's encounter with the proconsul Sergius Paulus at Paphos.

Source: Associates for Biblical Research · Associated Press

August 2026El-Araj, Sea of Galilee · early first century AD

Lamps, chalkstone vessels, and fishing weights at a Galilean site

In its tenth season, the El-Araj team reported intact Herodian lamps made in Jerusalem before AD 70, pieces of chalkstone vessels in every excavated square, and many fishing weights. Director R. Steven Notley argues the finds describe a community that was not poor, with fishing as a major industry. The team identifies the site as Bethsaida, an identification not all scholars share.

Bible connection. Bethsaida was the hometown of Philip, Andrew, and Peter (John 1:44).

Source: El-Araj Excavation Project

June 2026Shiloh, Israel · Middle Bronze Age and Iron Age

Shiloh's eighth season: a large building wall and storage jars

The Associates for Biblical Research reported three results from the season. The team found the southern wall of a large monumental building at the north end of the site, which they tentatively link to the tabernacle. They also uncovered more walls and rooms in the northern gate complex, and three large storage jars filled with burned olives and wheat from the Middle Bronze Age. Other archaeologists read the evidence differently.

Bible connection. Shiloh held the tabernacle in the days of Eli and Samuel (1 Samuel 1–4).

Source: Associates for Biblical Research

June 2026Tel 'Eton, Israel · late 8th century BC

Did a buried standing stone follow Hezekiah's reform?

A study in the Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology by Avraham Faust of Bar-Ilan University examines a standing stone found at Tel 'Eton. It was deliberately covered by a stone platform before the end of the eighth century BC. Faust suggests this may reflect the religious reforms of King Hezekiah. Whether the stone's burial was caused by that reform is an interpretation, not a settled conclusion.

Bible connection. 2 Kings 18:4 says Hezekiah removed the high places and broke the sacred pillars.

Source: Bible Archaeology Report, June roundup

How to read this page

Colors show each country’s rank on the Open Doors World Watch List 2026. Open Doors is a Christian advocacy organization, and its ranks reflect its own scoring of pressure and violence. A country outside the top 50 is not necessarily free of restrictions. Maldives and Comoros are too small to show at this scale, so use the list above.

“Christian-majority” uses rough estimates of 50% or more. Estimates differ by source and year, and many secular nations in Europe now fall under half.

Each profile labels its statements. Law is what the law says. Practice is how it works day to day. Reported is what advocacy groups or media describe, which is often hard to verify independently. Context is background. The profiles are summaries drawn from public sources, mostly Open Doors and Wikipedia’s country articles, and some details date from earlier years. Check them against current reports from USCIRF and the U.S. State Department before relying on them, especially before advising anyone who travels or works in these countries.

For safety, this site does not name or locate believers who could be identified.

About

Teaching written by a working pastor

Who is behind this

Open Scroll Ministries is the teaching ministry of RJ Leek, a Presbyterian pastor in Florida. The studies are prepared so that pastors and lay leaders can walk into the room and teach with confidence.

Why “Open Scroll”

The name comes from Luke 4:16–21, where Jesus stands in the synagogue at Nazareth, unrolls the scroll of Isaiah, and reads it aloud to the people who had known him all his life. The scroll was already written. What changed that day was that it was opened, read, and heard. That is the work of these studies.

How the studies are built

  1. They begin with the words, in the original Hebrew or Greek where the words matter.
  2. They set the passage in the world of its first hearers.
  3. They ask what the author meant before they ask what the passage requires of us.

Faithful application rests on faithful reading. This is careful, reverent teaching that lets Scripture say what it says.

What you receive

Downloads come as editable PowerPoint and Word files with print-ready PDFs, so you can use a study as written or adapt it to your group. The video studies include MP4 teaching videos, slides, and handouts. The worship dramas and liturgies are ready to hand to your readers.
